## How to work through this deck

Follow the main implications before opening the backup. For each one, explain what enters, what comes out, and why the implication holds. Resolve doubts needed to justify that implication. Record supporting proofs and side questions separately; they do not automatically become new slides.

## Essential checkpoints

1. Why values and first derivatives are sufficient for an embedding.
2. Which bundle to insert into Nadel so its canonical factor cancels.
3. Why a closed weighted B-valued (n,0)-form is exactly a section of K_X tensor B tensor I(h).
4. Why the estimate yields an actual solution, through a bounded functional and Riesz representation.
5. Why subtracting the solution corrects the cutoff section and preserves its quotient data.
6. Why the pole coefficients n and n+1 give m_x and m_x².
7. Why a high power dominates the curvature error, with one exponent valid for all points and pairs.
8. Why H1 vanishing gives surjective evaluation.
9. Why the resulting values give an injective map and the first jets give an injective differential.
10. Why compactness gives an embedding and Chow gives polynomial equations.

## Named inputs

Weighted local exactness/fine resolution, weak Cauchy–Riemann regularity, singular-weight L2 existence, the standard Hilbert-space adjoint/Riesz facts, and Chow are used with the hypotheses stated on the slides. Their supporting details are retained in the backup. Inner products in the Hilbert-space argument are linear in the first variable. The exact meaning of the estimates and the orthogonal projection is an essential question if not understood; a complete domain justification is in notes-backup.md.
