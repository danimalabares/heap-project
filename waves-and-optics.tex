\section{Following a crest}
Shake one end of a taut string. A marked
point on the string moves up and down as the
disturbance travels along it. For a
right-moving sinusoidal wave one can write
$$
y(x,t)=A\cos(kx-\omega t).
$$
A crest follows a line of constant phase,
hence its speed is $\omega/k$. The wavelength
is the distance between successive crests at
one instant.

\section{Why two slits give dark fringes}
Illuminate two narrow slits with the same
monochromatic light. At a point on a distant
screen the electric fields from both paths
add. Suppose they start in phase and reach
the point with equal amplitude and
polarization. A path difference of one
wavelength puts crest on crest. A difference
of half a wavelength puts crest on trough and
gives zero resultant field there. Moving
along the screen changes the path difference
and produces alternating bright and dark
fringes.

See \cite[Vol. I, Sections 29-3 to 29-5 and
47-2 to 47-4]{feynman-lectures}.
