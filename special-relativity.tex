\section{Timing two events}
A clock on a train emits a pulse of light
toward a mirror directly overhead. Its return
to the clock is a second event. In the train
frame the mirror is a distance $L$ away and
the elapsed time on the clock is
$\Delta\tau=2L/c$.

\section{The light clock from the platform}
Let the train move uniformly at speed $v$
relative to a platform. In the platform frame
the pulse follows two diagonal paths. During
half the round trip it travels a distance
$c\Delta t/2$, while the train moves $v\Delta
t/2$ horizontally. The transverse mirror
separation is still $L$. Pythagoras gives
$$
(c\Delta t/2)^2=L^2+(v\Delta t/2)^2.
$$
Substituting the reading of the train clock
gives
$$
\Delta t=
\frac{\Delta\tau}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}.
$$
At $v=3c/5$ the platform clocks assign five
seconds to a round trip taking four seconds
on the train clock. The two events occur at
different places on the platform, so its
elapsed time uses synchronized clocks.

See \cite[Vol. I, Sections 15-1 to
15-4]{feynman-lectures}.
