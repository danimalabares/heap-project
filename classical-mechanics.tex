\section{What determines a trajectory?}
To predict where a cart will be a second from
now, record its position and velocity and
specify the forces acting on it. In an
inertial frame a cart of constant mass $m$
obeys
$$
m\ddot x=F.
$$
A force determines acceleration. Two carts
subject to the same force can pass the same
point at different speeds.

\section{Releasing a spring}
Attach the cart to a spring on a frictionless
track. Measure $x$ from equilibrium. A
displacement $x$ produces a restoring force
$-kx$, where $k>0$. Pull the cart to $a$ and
release it from rest. Its motion is
$$
x(t)=a\cos(\omega t),\qquad
\omega=\sqrt{k/m}.
$$
Differentiating twice gives the force law.
The initial position fixes the amplitude and
the initial velocity fixes the phase. At
equilibrium the spring exerts zero force, yet
the cart passes through at its greatest
speed. Its initial spring energy $ka^2/2$ has
become kinetic energy.

See \cite[Vol. I, Sections 21-2 and
21-4]{feynman-lectures}.
