\section{Scope and sources}
\label{section-anthropology-scope}

This notebook brings together readings about
Indigenous peoples of Brazil. These are
situated study notes, not a claim to
original ethnographic fieldwork. Statements
about a people or a language require
separate evidence.

The Indigenous-language classification is
developed in Linguistics. Here the emphasis
is on peoples, social practices, and the
limits of the observer's knowledge. The
Bahia visit notes are in the chapter
Candombl\'e.

\section{Awa Guajá}
\label{section-awa-guaja}

The Awa Guajá live in Maranhão. The ISA
entry distinguishes village communities
from groups living in voluntary isolation
in the Awá, Caru and Araribóia Indigenous
Territories. Its sections ``Nomes'' and
``Língua'' explain the community spelling
``Awa'' and the language's membership in
Tupi-Guarani, within Tupi
\cite{isa-awa-guaja}.

Guajá and Guajajara are distinct names
for distinct peoples. Shared linguistic
affiliation does not make their histories
or social institutions interchangeable.
The classification is set out in
Linguistics,
Section~\ref{%
linguistics-section-indigenous-languages}.

On November 16, 2026, isolated Awa
approached a Guajajara village in
Arariboia. Federal agencies activated
protection and health protocols. Their
policy of non-contact emphasizes that
a single approach is not consent to
permanent contact
\footnote{Ministry of Indigenous Peoples,
press statement, November 17, 2026,
\url{https://www.gov.br/povosindigenas/pt-br/nota-a-imprensa}.}.
This dated report is a source for the
contact episode, not for linguistic
classification.


\section{Pataxó and Pataxó Hã-Hã-Hãe}
\label{section-pataxo}

The ISA entry locates Pataxó communities
in southern Bahia and northern Minas
Gerais. Its section on language describes
Patxohã as a collective language-recovery
process, including teaching and research
by Pataxó participants
\cite{isa-pataxo}.

Pataxó Hã-Hã-Hãe is an encompassing
ethnonym involving several historically
distinct groups. The ISA entry describes
dispossession and forced displacement
in southern Bahia
\cite{isa-pataxo-hahahae}.
This collective identity cannot be drawn
as a single daughter language of Pataxó.

\section{Fulni-ô: language and territory}
\label{section-fulni-o}

Jorge Hernández Díaz's ISA entry locates
the Fulni-ô in Águas Belas, Pernambuco.
It distinguishes Ia-tê from the Kariri
languages and describes the importance
of the Ouricuri to collective life
\cite{isa-fulni-o}.

The entry's account of language use and
population belongs to its reporting
period; it is not a current census.
Only its publicly documented account is
summarized here. The restricted contents
of the ritual are not reconstructed.

Daniel's notes of 25 May 2026 also raised
questions about land pressure, livelihoods
and language transmission. The source's
sections on historical background, economy
and social organization provide starting
points for those questions. They should
be read as dated accounts rather than
timeless descriptions of a people.
