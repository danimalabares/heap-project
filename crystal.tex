\section{Mumford degenerations}
\label{section-mumford-degenerations}

\noindent
The first object to understand is a family of
abelian varieties that degenerates. In the
simplest case the smooth fibers are elliptic
curves. Mumford's construction starts by
writing the smooth fibers as quotients of a
simpler space by a lattice action. Instead of
trying to take the limit of this quotient
directly, one first enlarges the simpler space
over the missing parameter value using toric
geometry, in such a way that the lattice
action still makes sense, and only then takes
the quotient. The combinatorial data used for
the toric enlargement is a periodic tiling.
Thus the rough pattern is: start with the
smooth quotient, extend its intermediate
cover using toric geometry, and then quotient
the extension. Here toric geometry means
building spaces from monomials and algebraic
tori; we will introduce the needed part when
it first enters the construction. For the
Tate curve this turns a family of
smooth elliptic curves into a family whose
central fiber is a nodal rational curve.

\medskip\noindent
{\bf The two-step quotient.}
Fix
$$
\tau\in\mathbb{C},
\qquad
\operatorname{Im}(\tau)>0.
$$
The corresponding elliptic curve is
$$
E_\tau
=
\mathbb{C}/
(\mathbb{Z}+\tau\mathbb{Z}).
$$
The subgroup
$\mathbb{Z}+\tau\mathbb{Z}$ is the lattice
generated by $1$ and $\tau$. Instead of
dividing by both generators at once, first
divide only by translations by integers:
$$
\mathbb{C}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}.
$$
The map
$$
z\longmapsto e^{2\pi i z}
$$
is unchanged when $z$ is replaced by $z+n$,
for $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, and it identifies
$$
\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}
\cong
\mathbb{C}^*.
$$
Here
$$
\mathbb{C}^*
=
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}.
$$

\medskip\noindent
Now define
$$
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}.
$$
Since $\operatorname{Im}(\tau)>0$, we have
$0<|q|<1$. Translation by $\tau$ upstairs,
$$
z\longmapsto z+\tau,
$$
becomes multiplication by $q$ downstairs:
$$
e^{2\pi i(z+\tau)}
=
q e^{2\pi i z}.
$$
Therefore the remaining group
$\tau\mathbb{Z}$ acts on
$\mathbb{C}^*$ by
$$
w\longmapsto q^n w,
\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}.
$$
This explains the notation
$$
q^{\mathbb{Z}}
=
\{q^n:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
$$
and
$$
\mathbb{C}^*/q^{\mathbb{Z}}.
$$
The latter means the quotient of
$\mathbb{C}^*$ by the equivalence relation
$$
w\sim q^n w,
\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}.
$$
Consequently,
$$
\boxed{
\mathbb{C}/
(\mathbb{Z}+\tau\mathbb{Z})
\cong
\mathbb{C}^*/q^{\mathbb{Z}}
}.
$$

\medskip\noindent
This is useful for degeneration because now
$q$, rather than $\tau$, can be used as the
parameter. Let $q$ vary in a punctured disk.
For every $q\neq0$ the fiber is
$$
E_q=\mathbb{C}^*/q^{\mathbb{Z}}.
$$
Thus the family of lattices is simply the
fact that the subgroup by which we divide
depends on the parameter:
$$
\mathbb{Z}+\tau\mathbb{Z}
$$
in the additive description, or
$$
q^{\mathbb{Z}}
$$
in the multiplicative description.

\medskip\noindent
The expression
$\mathbb{C}^*/q^{\mathbb{Z}}$ itself makes
no sense at $q=0$, because multiplication by
$q$ is no longer an automorphism of
$\mathbb{C}^*$. This is exactly where the
Mumford construction begins. One first
extends the intermediate cover
$\mathbb{C}^*$ over $q=0$ by a toric space
on which the periodic $\mathbb{Z}$-action
still extends, and then takes the quotient.
For the Tate curve the central fiber of the
cover is an infinite periodic chain of copies
of $\mathbb{P}^1$. Quotienting by the
translation by one period identifies this
chain to one copy of $\mathbb{P}^1$ with
$0$ and $\infty$ identified, namely a nodal
rational curve. The periodic subdivision of
the real line recording this chain is the
first appearance of the tiling in Mumford's
construction.
