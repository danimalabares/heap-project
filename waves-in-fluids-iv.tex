\section{Before the meeting}

How does the depth of a channel affect the
speed of a long surface wave? Consider an
inviscid fluid in a straight channel with
a flat bed. Write $h$ for the water depth
at rest, $\eta(x,t)$ for the displacement
of the surface, and $v(x,t)$ for the
depth-averaged horizontal velocity.

Suppose the displacement is small compared
with $h$ and the wavelength is long
compared with $h$. The linear shallow
water equations in one dimension are
$$
\eta_t=-h v_x,\qquad v_t=-g\eta_x.
$$
The first equation expresses conservation
of water: if more flows out of a short
stretch than flows in, its surface falls.
The second says that a surface slope
accelerates water downhill under gravity.

Differentiate the first equation in time
and substitute the second. This gives
$$
\eta_{tt}=gh\,\eta_{xx}.
$$
A pulse of the form $\eta(x,t)=F(x-ct)$
solves this equation for $c=\sqrt{gh}$.
In this model, quadrupling the depth
doubles the speed of the pulse, provided
the shallow water approximation still
holds.
