What this is

The surface of a convex polyhedron is a 2-sphere made of faces. Remove a vertex v: what is left of the sphere is a plane, and this is a picture of it. It is the 3D models, one dimension down.

On the right is the solid itself, with the same colours. Look from v turns it so that v points at you: then it sits exactly as the picture on the left, which is what the solid looks like from v. The steering wheel turns it about the line of sight. Each face that touches v has its own colour there; in the picture it is the region with that colour outside the rim, between two rays. It goes on to infinity; the colour is cut where the rays are cut.

The thick curve is the rim: the far edges of the faces that touch v. When all faces are triangles it is the link of v. The faces that do not touch v fill the inside of the rim. The faces that touch v are the outside: one region between each pair of neighbouring green rays, and the rays are the edges going to v, which is at infinity.

Round. Put the vertices on a round sphere and project stereographically from v. Edges are arcs of circles and the rays are literal.

Straight. Look at the polyhedron from a point just outside it, near v, and draw the far side. Edges are straight and the rim is a convex polygon. The rays are a convention here: they point away from the centre.

The other triangulations are not regular, so different vertices give different pictures: use the arrows. The last three are links of vertices in the two 3-spheres of the 4D tab that are not boundaries of convex polytopes: the link of b in M, and the links of 7 and of 1 in Barnette's sphere, with the names of the vertices. The link of 7 is an octahedron.
