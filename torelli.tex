\section{Background already in the Heap}
\label{section-background}

This section is a guide to existing notes.
Read it from the reconstruction question
to the Hodge-theoretic data, then to the
examples. Definitions, proofs and source
references remain in their original
chapters; the links below gather them here.
This is an index, not a new verification
of the statements in those notes.

\medskip\noindent
{\bf The starting question.}
Which data attached to an object remember
its isomorphism class? For the period-map
formulation already in the Heap, begin at
\ref{variations-of-hodge-structures-%
section-period-maps}.
Keep track of what data are retained:
a lattice, its pairing, its Hodge
decomposition, and any additional marking
or polarization specified in the theorem.

\medskip\noindent
{\bf From forms to cohomology.}
For the analytic background, see
\ref{hodge-theory-theorem-hodge-spectral},
\ref{hodge-theory-theorem-hodge-de-rham}
and
\ref{hodge-theory-theorem-hodge-dolbeault}.
The complementary treatment is in
\ref{complex-geometry-%
section-hodge-theory}, leading to
\ref{complex-geometry-theorem-hodge-theorem}.
These are the existing entries on harmonic
representatives and Hodge decomposition.

\medskip\noindent
{\bf The familiar K3 example.}
Start with the lattice and period in
\ref{k3-definition-k3-lattice},
\ref{k3-definition-period-k3-surface} and
\ref{k3-%
definition-period-domain-k3-surfaces}.
The local discussion is
\ref{k3-section-local-torelli}, with the
period map defined in
\ref{k3-definition-period-map-misha}.
For the existing global Torelli statement,
see
\ref{complex-geometry-theorem-torelli-k3}.
Read its hypotheses together with the
Hodge-isometry formulation in
\ref{cremona-transformations-%
theorem-global-torelli-k3}.
Distinguish reconstructing an isomorphism
class from realizing a specified
cohomology isometry by a geometric map.
