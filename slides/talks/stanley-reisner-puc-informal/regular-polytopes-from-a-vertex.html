What this is

A regular 4-polytope sits in 4-space with its vertices on a round 3-sphere. Its boundary is a 3-sphere made of cells. All vertices look the same, so one vertex v is enough.

Round. Push the boundary out onto the round 3-sphere and project stereographically from v. Then v is at infinity, the neighbours of v (the link) lie on a round 2-sphere, edges are arcs of circles, and the edges through v are straight rays going out. The cells that do not touch v fill the inside; the cells that touch v are the outside.

Straight. Look at the polytope from a point just outside it, near v, and draw only the far side. Everything is flat, the link of v is a convex polyhedron, and the cells that do not touch v tile it. This picture exists because the polytope is convex. The edges going to v are drawn as straight rays pointing away from the centre: with them, the cells that touch v are the regions outside, between the rays.

Cells are filled only when they are tetrahedra (5-cell, 16-cell, 600-cell). The slider adds them starting from the ones nearest to v.
