\section{AI4MATH seminar at IMPA:

Notes from the introductory AI4MATH
seminar at IMPA on October 20, 2026,
based on slides 3, 4, 5 and 7.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{The OpenAI--IMPA collaboration.}
In August 2026 IMPA announced that Centro Pi
had been selected for one of fourteen
independent OpenAI-supported projects
worldwide, from over 400 applications. Its
project, \emph{Building Resilient Scientific
Research in the Age of Intelligence}, studies
how mathematical institutions can use AI
efficiently, reliably, sustainably, and
equitably. Topics include infrastructure,
computing, engineering support, training,
governance and costs. The six-month project
is led by Daniel Yukimura; the announced team
also includes Francisco Ganacim,
Jo\~ao Pedro Ramos, and Cynthia Bortolotto.
OpenAI's support includes funding and API
credits. The project plans to publish an
assessment of the costs, infrastructure and
responsible use of AI, potentially applicable
to other institutions.

IMPA also advertised up to six master's or
doctoral technological development
fellowships, with activities scheduled from
September 2026 to March 2027. These include
AI-assisted mathematics, agent development,
evaluation tools, formalization with Lean,
literature review, reports and seminars.

\textbf{Official abstract (verbatim).}
This is the first session of a biweekly
seminar series from the AI4MATH project
(IMPA--OpenAI). Students in the project
present the open problems they have been
working on with AI assistance, and the
results they obtained. We will discuss how
AI tools can help mathematical research,
where they fall short, and what difficulties
came up along the way. This week discussion
will be based on the following problems:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The Khavinson--Shapiro
conjecture:} do polynomial Dirichlet data
characterize ellipsoids? (Steinerberger)
\item \textbf{Additive energy on discrete
grids} $\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^d$ and a sharp
$L^4$ inequality (Madrid)
\item \textbf{Which radius does the
Hardy--Littlewood maximal function select?}
(Steinerberger)
\item \textbf{Cauchy--Davenport from small
subsets:} is the bound witnessed by subsets
of size $O(\sqrt{n})$? (Tiba)
\end{enumerate}

The session is open to everyone at IMPA, and
feedback from researchers and students is
very welcome.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Organization and purpose of the
seminar.}
Each student works on one open problem for
two weeks using an AI assistant. They then
present the mathematics obtained, including
partial and negative results, and discuss how
the work went, including where the assistant
helped and where it did not. The objective is
not necessarily to solve the problems but to
generate better mathematical understanding.

The assistant will be developed over time
according to students' experience and seminar
feedback. Successful and failed approaches
will be recorded to clarify how AI can and
cannot contribute to mathematical research.
The seminar is intended as a place for open,
careful and critical discussion of
mathematics, methods and limitations. It
meets every other Tuesday and is open to
everyone at IMPA.
